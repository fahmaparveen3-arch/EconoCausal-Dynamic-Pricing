# Profit Analysis

## 1. Objective

The objective of this analysis is to understand how different discount levels may affect profitability in the EconoCausal Dynamic Pricing project.

The analysis considers customer purchasing behaviour, revenue, and product costs to help evaluate pricing decisions.

## 2. Revenue and Profit

Revenue represents the money generated from sales. Profit depends on revenue and the associated costs.

A simplified calculation is:

**Profit = Revenue − Product Costs − Discount Costs − Other Applicable Costs**

The exact calculation must follow the definitions implemented in the project's evaluation code to avoid double-counting discounts or costs.

## 3. Discount-Level Comparison

The project considers five discount levels:

- 0%
- 5%
- 10%
- 15%
- 20%

For each level, the analysis should compare:

- Number of customers.
- Purchase rate.
- Average revenue per customer.
- Average profit per customer.
- Total profit, if the groups have comparable sizes or results are appropriately normalised.

## 4. Interpreting the Results

A higher discount may encourage more purchases, but it can also reduce the amount earned on each sale.

For example, a discount group could have a higher purchase rate but lower profit per customer than a group receiving a smaller discount.

The project’s current evaluation reports that the 20% discount group has the highest purchase rate, while the 15% discount group has the highest profit per customer among the discounted groups.

These findings must be checked against the latest evaluation output before being treated as confirmed results.

## 5. Pricing Decision

A suitable pricing decision should consider profitability rather than purchase rate alone.

The recommended discount should be selected using the project's profit metric, subject to appropriate business constraints and reliable evaluation.

## 6. Limitations

The dataset is synthetic, and the profit evaluation depends on the cost assumptions used in the project.

The results therefore demonstrate an analytical approach rather than proving how real customers or businesses would respond to a particular discount.

## 7. Conclusion

Profit analysis helps identify whether additional purchases generated by discounts compensate for the reduction in revenue per sale.

Comparing purchase rate, revenue, and profit together provides a more useful basis for evaluating discount strategies than considering conversion alone.
